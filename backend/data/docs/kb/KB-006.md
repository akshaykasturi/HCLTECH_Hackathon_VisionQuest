# Office wireless connectivity problems

## Symptoms and scope

Laptops fail to join the office wireless network, drop the connection, or get poor throughput in some areas. Causes include drivers, authentication, coverage and congestion.

This guide covers these known causes:

- Outdated wireless driver with aggressive roaming behaviour (typical signal: `roam failure reason 8`)
- Device missing the machine certificate used for wireless authentication (typical signal: `EAP-TLS failure`)
- Coverage gap after office layout change (typical signal: `RSSI -82 dBm`)
- Channel congestion and too many clients on the 2.4 GHz band (typical signal: `channel utilisation 85%`)
- Wireless DHCP scope out of free addresses (typical signal: `DHCP scope 100% used`)

Synthetic guidance written for development and evaluation; it is not the approved procedure of any real organisation.

## Before you start

You need read access to the wireless controller and the device management console. Do not change access point settings without a change record; collect the device name and location first.

Record the ticket ID, the affected users or systems, the start time and any recent changes before you act. Do not assume a cause from symptoms alone: match the observed signal to a cause below.

## Diagnostic checks

- **Outdated wireless driver with aggressive roaming behaviour.** When one model drops while roaming, compare the wireless driver version with the supported baseline and read roaming events on the controller.
- **Device missing the machine certificate used for wireless authentication.** If one device cannot join the corporate network, check the authentication log for the device and whether the machine certificate is present.
- **Coverage gap after office layout change.** For problems tied to one area, read the signal strength the controller reports for clients there and compare with the coverage plan.
- **Channel congestion and too many clients on the 2.4 GHz band.** When slowness happens at busy times, check channel utilisation and client count per radio for the access points in that area.
- **Wireless DHCP scope out of free addresses.** If clients connect but receive self-assigned addresses, check the free address count in the DHCP scope for that wireless network.

## Resolution steps by cause

### Outdated wireless driver with aggressive roaming behaviour

1. Check the wireless driver version against the supported baseline.
2. Deploy the supported wireless driver from the device management console.
3. Restart the laptop after the driver update.
4. Confirm the controller shows no roaming failures for the device.

### Device missing the machine certificate used for wireless authentication

1. Confirm the authentication server log shows a certificate failure.
2. Trigger machine certificate enrollment through the device management console.
3. Reconnect the laptop to the corporate network.
4. Confirm the authentication log shows success.

### Coverage gap after office layout change

1. Record client signal strength in the affected area from the controller.
2. Request a site survey for the affected area.
3. Raise a change to add or move an access point based on the survey.
4. Confirm signal strength meets the coverage target.

### Channel congestion and too many clients on the 2.4 GHz band

1. Check channel utilisation and client count on the affected access points.
2. Enable band steering so capable clients move to 5 GHz.
3. Review channel plan changes with Network Operations.
4. Confirm utilisation drops during the next busy period.

### Wireless DHCP scope out of free addresses

1. Confirm the DHCP scope for the wireless network has no free addresses.
2. Shorten the lease time for the guest scope through change control.
3. Raise a change to extend the scope with Network Operations.
4. Confirm new devices receive an address.

## Validation and escalation

Confirm the device stays connected for a working session in the reported area and that the controller shows a healthy signal and no authentication failures.

Escalate to Network Operations if a whole floor or building is affected, or if access points are offline.

Also escalate when the observed facts match none of the causes above, when a step fails, or when the impact grows while you work. Record each step taken and its result in the ticket.
